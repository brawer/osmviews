\subsection{Agreement with other data}\label{sec:external}
Where people look on maps should follow where people live and what they
visit. We compare OSMViews with three independent measures: population in
2025 from GHS-POP~\cite{ghsl}, night-light radiant flux in 2024 derived from
VIIRS~\cite{viirs}, and the camera locations of photos on Wikimedia Commons.
All are brought onto the pixels of the overview level with
\resExternalLevel{} pixels across the world (about 10\,km at the equator).
Gridded data is turned into a density per km\textsuperscript{2} and
max-pooled onto these pixels, just like the overview levels of the
GeoTIFF~(\S\ref{sec:construction}); photos are counted per pixel and divided
by its area. Correlations are Spearman's, over the
\resExternalLandKm{}\,km\textsuperscript{2} of land outside
Antarctica~\cite{naturalearth}, with each pixel weighted by its ground area
so that Web Mercator does not inflate high latitudes.

OSMViews correlates with population at \resExternalGhsl{}, with night
lights at \resExternalViirs{} and with photo locations at
\resExternalCommons{} (Table~\ref{tab:external}). That is about as strong as
population and night lights agree with each other
(\resExternalGhslViirs{}), and stronger than either agrees with photo
locations. By continent (Table~\ref{tab:continents}), agreement is highest
in Europe and North America and lowest in Africa.
\todo{Interpret: Africa is where most lanes run (\S\ref{sec:filter}); rerun
with the filtered raster once production publishes it, and report regular
vs.\ filtered side by side. Population counts residents, OSMViews also
counts visitors (tourist regions, transport corridors).}

\subsection{Downstream use}
QRank ranks Wikidata items by their page views across Wikimedia
projects~\cite{qrank}. For the \resQrankItems{} items that have both a
coordinate location and a QRank, we read OSMViews at the item's coordinates
on the level with \resQrankLevel{} pixels across the world (about 600\,m at
the equator). The coordinates come from the Wikidata database dumps. The
rank correlation between QRank and OSMViews is \resQrankSpearman{}, and
\resQrankWithViewsPct\,\% of the items lie on pixels with views.
\todo{Interpret: QRank measures interest in a thing, OSMViews in a place, so
a moderate correlation is expected. Once the filtered raster is published:
items on lanes vs.\ a matched control set. OSMDiffs: whether OSMViews helps
rank edit proposals, once the list of reviewed proposals is in.}

\subsection{Stability and coverage}
Each release covers a trailing year of tile logs, so consecutive weekly
releases share all but one week. Across the \resStabilityReleases{} latest
releases, the area-weighted rank correlation between consecutive releases is
at least \resStabilitySpearmanMin{}, and at least
\resStabilityTopKeptPctMin\,\% of the top \resStabilityTopPct\,\% of pixels
stay in the top \resStabilityTopPct\,\%.
\todo{Stability of single weeks, which needs per-week rasters from the
builder; effect of partial weeks.}

\resCoverageInCountriesPct\,\% of all views fall on a country; the rest is
mostly sea. Across the \resCoverageCountries{} countries with recent World
Bank figures~\cite{wdi} (latest year since 2020, median
\resCoverageMedianYear{}), a country's views correlate with its number of
internet users at \resCoverageSpearman{} (Spearman).
Table~\ref{tab:coverage} lists the countries with at least
\resCoverageMinUsersMillions{} million internet users that have the most and
fewest views per internet user, relative to the world.
